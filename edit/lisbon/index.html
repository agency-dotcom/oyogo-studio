Location edit

Lisbon

Eat · Drink · Move · Recover · Explore ≈ 5 min read

This isn't a tourist guide – it's our wellness travel edit. Where to move, train, recover and eat well during your stay in Lisbon.

Where to stay

Torel Palace

Perched on one of Lisbon's oldest hilltops inside a restored palace, Torel Palace brings a beautifully considered wellness offering into the heart of the city. Hammam rituals, organic Portuguese treatments, hilltop yoga and panoramic views of the Tagus included.

Where to move + recover

Somewhere to stretch, strengthen and sweat

The best places to move and recover across Lisbon.

The Kynd Space

Reformer Pilates, mat classes, yoga, barre and HIIT, all taught in English and designed around a space that feels as good to be in as the classes themselves.

Amplify

Indoor cycling, bootcamp, yoga and barre across three city locations. The cycling classes are their signature: soulful, music-driven and full body.

Studio Rise

The best cycling studio in town. Intense indoor cycling in meditative half-light, set to a soundtrack that makes it feel less like a workout and more like a night out.

Contrast Club

Hot yoga, strength, breathwork and contrast therapy steps from Carcavelos beach. Infrared-heated classes, a smoothie bar, and a community that runs well beyond the studio floor.

Where to eat well

Eating well while you travel should feel effortless

Our favourite healthy cafés, local restaurants and nourishing places to eat.

Honest Greens

Fresh, fast and genuinely good. Bowls and plates built around seasonal ingredients, with options for every diet.

Magnolia

A neighbourhood bistro for brunch, small plates and natural wine in Praça das Flores, with an organic farmers market every Thursday.

Aura Wellness Bar

Cold-pressed juices, smoothies and wellness shots built around whole ingredients and adaptogens. Functional and delicious.

Pomme Eatery

Seasonal, organic small plates and natural wines in Príncipe Real. Order everything and share it.

Where to run, walk and explore

Lisbon rewards those who move through it on foot

An early morning run through Alfama's empty streets, a run along the Tagus, or a slow climb between miradouros as the city wakes up. Lisbon is the city of seven hills, so if you like some incline you're in the right place – and it has some of the most beautiful architecture, so make sure you look up.

Tagus Riverfront

A flat, wide path running west from the city centre to Belém. Open skies, river views and almost no traffic.

Best at sunrise

Alfama

Steep, narrow streets threading past tiled facades and sleeping fado bars.

Run it early, before the city wakes

Monsanto

2,400 acres of forested trails on the city's western edge. Hilly, quiet and surprisingly wild for a capital city.

Cascais Esplanade

A coastal path running west from Estoril along the Atlantic shoreline to Cascais – wide, flat and open to the ocean.
